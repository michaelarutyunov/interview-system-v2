# ZeroFizz Sugar-Free Carbonated Beverage — Domain Brief

**Bundle:** `domain:zerofizz_beverage_v1`
**Status:** Immutable. Revisions become v2, not silent edits to v1.
**Scope:** Methodology-agnostic source of truth for the 5 matched concept YAMLs in `config/concepts/zerofizz_beverage_{mec,jtbd,cit,cjm,rg}.yaml`.

---

## Respondent population

Urban and suburban U.S. adults 18–35, moderate disposable income, digital-native, habitual carbonated-beverage consumers (≥2–3 per week). Varying health-consciousness: from gym-centered respondents who track macros to "I'd like to be healthier but I'm not strict" respondents who read labels sporadically. Have tried at least 3 different zero-sugar or functional beverages in the past 12 months. Follow at least one fitness, wellness, or food creator on TikTok or Instagram.

## Occasions of use

- Weekday 2–4pm desk slump, where another coffee feels excessive.
- Post-workout, substituting for an electrolyte drink or as a small earned reward.
- Pre-event alcohol pacing (pre-dinner, pre-concert) when arriving tipsy is undesirable.
- Sober-curious social settings — rooftop bars, house parties, weddings — where ordering a ZeroFizz rather than a cocktail or beer preserves an adult-coded drink in hand.
- Meal accompaniment with takeout or casual dining where plain water feels low-effort.
- Long focus sessions: gaming, studying, deep-work stretches of 2–3 hours.
- Gas-station impulse purchase on road trips.

## Purchase contexts

Weekly grocery 12-pack, convenience-store single can, employer-stocked office fridge, bar or restaurant mocktail menu, gym vending machine, delivery-app add-on.

## What they care about — functional

Genuinely zero sugar (distrust of "low sugar" hedging). Specific sweetener identity — sucralose, stevia, allulose, ace-K — with strong aftertaste opinions. Caffeine dose stated clearly and mentally benchmarked against coffee (~95mg) and Red Bull (~80mg). Calorie count at or near zero, operating as "free food" in their mental ledger. Short, recognizable ingredient labels — "natural flavors" alone is a yellow flag. Carbonation intensity (some want aggressive fizz, some prefer softer). Can design: matte, bold, typographic signals newness; glossy red or blue signals "old soda." Price per can $1.50–3.00 acceptable, above $3 needs justification. Availability at their usual grocery — if it's not on the shelf reliably, they won't switch permanently.

## What they care about — emotional / identity

"I'm the kind of person who reads labels, tries new things, doesn't need sugar for enjoyment." Modernity — the drink should belong to their generation, not their parents'. Indulgence without penalty: carbonation scratches the soda itch, zero calories removes the guilt. Alertness without jitters — energy that feels clean, not chemical. Confidence in social settings where not drinking alcohol would otherwise feel conspicuous.

## What they care about — social

Being seen drinking something deliberate, not default. Matching peer behavior (friends doing Dry January, training for a race, cutting back during the week). Not being the only non-drinker — having an adult-coded beverage in hand at a bar. Signaling alignment with fitness and wellness subcultures without preaching. Sharing recommendations on TikTok and Instagram as cultural currency.

## Experience arc

- **Discovery** — TikTok ad, a friend's can at a picnic, endcap at Trader Joe's or Whole Foods, gym-buddy reference.
- **Trial** — single-can purchase; first 30 seconds decide the taste verdict; aftertaste verdict comes 2–3 minutes later.
- **Evaluation** — second can within the week is the positive signal; no second can within 2 weeks is effectively rejection.
- **Adoption** — 12-pack purchase, favorite flavor identified, cadence settles (daily, every-other-day, weekends).
- **Switching** — triggered by aftertaste fatigue, a competitor launch, or a friend's stronger recommendation.
- **Abandonment** — often ingredient panic (a creator calls out sucralose), a price increase, or an availability drop.
- **Re-adoption** — new flavor launch, reformulation news, seasonal FOMO.

## Contrast set and mental distinctions

- **Coke Zero / Pepsi Zero** — tastes like soda, trusted, boring, uncool.
- **LaCroix / Spindrift / Bubly** — "healthy" but unsatisfying when the craving is a real soda.
- **Red Bull / Celsius / Alani Nu / Ghost** — energy-forward, often over-caffeinated, sometimes "too bro" or "too gym-girly."
- **Olipop / Poppi** — prebiotic functional soda, gut-health coded, premium priced.
- **Kombucha** — acquired taste, alcohol-adjacent fizz, probiotic story.
- **Plain water / sparkling water** — baseline hydration, "boring," social-awkwardness vehicle.
- **Regular soda** — what they grew up on, now framed as unhealthy or immature.

## Behavioral tensions

- Want indulgence + reject sugar → artificial sweeteners are the compromise, but distrust of sweeteners shifts (allulose rising, aspartame falling).
- Want energy + reject "energy drink" aesthetic → a soda-coded energy drink occupies this middle.
- Want lifestyle belonging + distrust lifestyle marketing → they can smell an ad written by a 50-year-old CMO and they punish it.
- Want healthy identity + enjoy consumption → the drink must feel earned, not medicinal.
- Want novelty + want reliability → they'll try the new launch but abandon quickly if it's inconsistent.
- Want social ease when others are drinking alcohol + don't want to explain why they aren't.

## Values the category connects to

- **Autonomy** — I choose what I put in my body.
- **Self-control** — I can enjoy carbonation and caffeine without the downstream costs.
- **Vitality** — I want to feel alert, ready, energetic.
- **Belonging** — I want to be at the table, drink in hand, part of the moment.
- **Identity** — this says something about who I am.
- **Authenticity** — the brand should feel made for me, not at me.
- **Pragmatism** — I make tradeoffs; zero-sugar is a reasonable compromise, not a purity test.

## Edge populations (handled at persona layer, not in concept objectives)

- Pregnant or pregnancy-adjacent respondents (zero-sugar for clinical reasons).
- Diabetic / pre-diabetic respondents (clinical, not lifestyle).
- Fitness respondents treating caffeine as performance-enhancing.
- Sober / sober-curious respondents using the beverage as an alcohol substitute.
- Parents reducing sugar for modeling behavior toward their kids.
